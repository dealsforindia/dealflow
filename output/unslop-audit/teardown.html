The products should lead.
Right now, the interface leads.

The site already has content density, real imagery and useful shopping features. Its unfinished feel comes from weak priority between elements, repeated decoration and inconsistent treatment of the same information.

Live dark-mode homepage reviewed at 1280 × 720 and 393 × 852. Numbered markers identify observed issues; recommendations are design judgement, not measured conversion findings.

Desktop: too much introduction before the inventory

12345

1 Too many header priorities

Discover, discounts, Saved, search, profile, appearance, Telegram and More all share the top band. Telegram receives stronger emphasis than finding products.

Give browsing and search the primary positions. Group account and appearance utilities; treat Telegram as a secondary destination.

2 The ticker adds a competing feed

A moving row of clipped offer titles asks for attention before the main inventory. The same offers appear elsewhere.

Keep a strip only if it adds a distinct, useful signal. Otherwise return that space to the feed.

3 The hero dominates the shopping task

The bordered blue hero is approximately 410px tall in this capture. Its slogan receives more room than the searchable inventory. The spotlight repeats an existing deal.

Compose search and a meaningful featured offer together, with a smaller introduction. A featured product should earn its space.

4 Every shortcut is a little panel

Search, trend counts, popular searches and merchant names accumulate inside an already framed section. Their treatments imply more independent features than their labels explain.

Use one clear search action; make suggestions lighter. Define what Price Radar does before giving it special styling.

5 Several layers of browsing controls

Collections, category navigation and category/store filters precede the grid. The Latest finds heading was observed around document y=842, below the 720px desktop viewport.

Give categories one clear home. Make collections earn their placement through actual product discovery rather than another chip row.

Deal cards: useful content, uneven visual weight

6789

6 Imagery needs consistent art direction

Cutout appliances, a lifestyle lamp photo and fashion photography have very different apparent scales within identical white wells. The frame is consistent; the product presence is not.

Set image-fit rules by image type. Keep photographs natural, standardize padding for cutouts and reserve useful subject area. Do not replace photos with icons.

7 Merchant titles are doing all the naming

Long scraped descriptions wrap or truncate, while shorter titles leave inconsistent whitespace. Key product identity and secondary specifications are fused.

Use a concise display name plus a separate specification line. Preserve the complete source title in details. Align the price and action areas.

8 Discount and evidence need different roles

The green below-MRP badge draws attention before the price. Ratings, checking-review states and checkout advice each add another row.

Lead with the actual price and clear comparison basis. Show available evidence compactly; keep unavailable/loading states neutral. A below-MRP percentage is not proof of a historical price drop.

9 Too many ways to leave the card

Image, title, reviews, merchant button, overflow and Details each have different destinations or behaviours. Every card repeats a large filled action.

Define the two main paths: inspect the deal or visit the merchant. Make their destinations clear, keep save easy to reach, and place occasional tools in overflow.

Mobile: more efficient layout, less consistent finishing

10111213

10 The header feels like a utility toolbar

Five adjacent icon controls sit beside the brand. Their similar shapes and styling make search, profile, appearance, Telegram and alerts look equally important.

Keep the most frequent tasks visible; consolidate the rest. Give each remaining control a clear role and consistent icon weight.

11 Multiple accent systems compete

Blue headline emphasis, blue controls, a gold Price Radar chip, green prices and green discount labels all appear in one screen. Raised edges and gradients reinforce each one.

Assign color by role. Reserve the strongest emphasis for the current primary action; keep evidence and merchandising accents quieter.

12 Desktop and mobile speak differently

Desktop says Latest finds with a DIRECTORY label; mobile says Latest Drops with a fire emoji. The mobile hero also uses different supporting copy.

Use one naming system and one evidence standard. Adapt layout and length to the screen while preserving meaning.

13 Persistent controls use substantial space

The scrolled capture keeps the header and categories at the top and a prominent five-item dock at the bottom. Search appears in the header, hero and raised dock action.

Choose where search lives in each scroll state. Reduce persistent chrome and verify that the last card actions remain reachable above the dock.

Keep these strengths

The mobile two-column feed gets products into the first screen. Real photography, prominent rupee prices, familiar merchant names and visible save controls are worth preserving. No whole-page horizontal overflow was measured in this sampled mobile state: scrollWidth 387px at a 393px viewport.

Content quality is part of the design

The feed contains both “Lifelong LLHFD442 Air Fryer” and “Lifelong LLHFD442 Air Fryer (4.2 L)” at ₹1,999 with the same MRP. They appear to be duplicate listings; confirm identifiers before merging. Truncated source titles and large MRP comparisons also need editorial treatment. Styling alone cannot make an inconsistent inventory feel curated.

The first redesign should prove three things

Hierarchy

On desktop, show a useful first row of products at the agreed target viewport. Search, categories and products should have an obvious reading order.

Craft

One coherent set of type sizes, surface levels, borders, radii and icons. Test long titles, mixed photography, missing ratings and large prices.

Identity

Develop an original IndiaDealHunts detail around product discovery and price comparison. Avoid decorative finance charts or dot-matrix styling borrowed from the reference videos.

Scope: visual and information hierarchy review of the live dark homepage. No storefront source changes or deployment. Conversion impact, full accessibility compliance and end-to-end interaction correctness were not tested. Additional scrolled mobile evidence.
